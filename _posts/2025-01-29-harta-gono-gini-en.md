## What Is Harta Gono-Gini?

**Harta gono-gini** refers to property acquired by a husband and wife during the marriage. The term comes from Javanese tradition, but Indonesian law recognizes it as *harta bersama* — joint marital property, the equivalent of *community property*. Examples include a house, a car, savings, or investments bought during the marriage. Property brought in before the marriage (*harta bawaan*) and any inheritance or gift declared to be personal property are **not** included in this category.

## The Legal Basis of Harta Gono-Gini

1. **Article 35 paragraph (1) of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage** — property acquired during the marriage automatically becomes joint property, unless there is a prenuptial agreement.
2. **Article 119 of the KUHPerdata (the Indonesian Civil Code)** — confirms that joint property applies from the moment of marriage, unless the parties agree otherwise in an agreement.
3. **The KHI (Kompilasi Hukum Islam, the Compilation of Islamic Law)** — Article 85 of the KHI recognizes joint property, but the division is handled proportionally (usually 50:50).

## Types of Property in a Marriage

1. **Pre-marital property (*harta bawaan*)** — owned before the marriage (for example, an inherited house).
2. **Separate property** — a gift or inheritance declared to be one spouse's personal property.
3. **Joint property (*harta gono-gini*)** — acquired during the marriage (for example, salary, or a jointly run business).

## How Harta Gono-Gini Is Divided

### On Divorce

- **Deliberation (*musyawarah*)** — a fair settlement reached by agreement, without going to court, is preferred.
- **Court** — if agreement fails, the judge divides the property 50:50, though the split can differ based on financial or non-financial contribution (for example, child-rearing).
- **Debt** — divided by agreement or by court decision, especially if it was used for the family's needs.

### On Death

Joint property becomes part of the estate. The heirs (children, spouse) are entitled to their shares under the inheritance law that applies — the Civil Code (KUHPerdata) or the KHI.

## Tips for Managing Harta Gono-Gini

1. **Make a prenuptial agreement (*perjanjian pranikah*)** to keep personal property separate.
2. **Be transparent about finances** — discuss income, spending, and investments openly.
3. **Avoid excessive debt** that burdens the joint property.
